Trente

A free 3D map for planning ski tours around Verbier.

Open the map

Why I built it

I ski in Verbier and used FATMAP to look at slopes in 3D before skiing them. Strava bought FATMAP in 2023 and shut it down on 1 October 2024. The apps people moved to didn't replace it for me, so I'm building my own, starting with the mountains I know best.

The name is French for thirty. Slab avalanches need a slope of at least 30° to be triggered, so the map colours every slope that steep. An avalanche can still run out onto the flatter ground below.

What the map does

  • 3D terrain

    Tilt and turn the mountains with two fingers. swisstopo's aerial photos are laid over the terrain.

  • Slopes over 30°

    swisstopo's slope layer colours every slope over 30°, in classes recommended by the SLF. The colour key is in the map.

  • Ski touring routes

    Switch on swisstopo's ski touring routes to see where tours usually go.

  • Route drawing

    Tap to add points and see the distance, the climb and descent, an elevation profile and the steepest part of your line.

  • GPX export

    Save your route as a GPX file for a GPS watch or another app.

How to use it

  1. Open the map and find your area. Pinch to zoom, and drag with two fingers to tilt and turn.

  2. Look at the slope colours, and switch on ski touring routes to see the usual lines.

  3. Tap Draw route, then tap along your line. Check the profile, then tap GPX to save it.

A planning aid, not avalanche advice

This map shows the shape of the mountain. It knows nothing about today's snow, so it can't tell you whether a slope is safe.

  • Read the SLF avalanche bulletin for your area before every tour.
  • Go with a mountain guide, or with partners trained in avalanche safety.
  • Carry a transceiver, shovel and probe, and know how to use them.

Know the map's limits

  • swisstopo works out the slope colours from a 10 m elevation model, so short steep pitches, gullies and cornices may not show.
  • Avalanches that start on steep slopes can run out onto flatter ground below, and slabs can be triggered from a distance.
  • "Steepest part" measures your line, not the slope around it. A traverse across a 40° slope can show 10°.
  • The aerial photos aren't live and don't show today's snow.
  • Route lines show where tours usually go, not that they're safe today.
